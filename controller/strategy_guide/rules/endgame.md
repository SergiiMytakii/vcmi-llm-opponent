# Finish the game

Compare complete elimination operations, not cheap fights in isolation: defeat the main army then remove remaining bases/forces, or deny all enemy towns for the public defeat period. Name the obstacle, next feasible step, protection against recapture and conditions for reassessment. Main-hero defeat alone is not victory; retreat, rehire, other heroes and towns can preserve opposition. Town capture alone starts no proven countdown when unseen holdings remain unknown.

Use public participants, elimination status and townless rules; enemy countdown or hidden holdings marked unknown stay unknown. Prevent a town reset through supported interception, garrisons or covered approaches, rather than repeatedly trading bases. A weak target or mine must materially support the chosen elimination operation.

Compare route loss components and visible count sensitivity: conservative upper estimates are not exact battle outcomes. A costly decisive attack may be worth more than indefinite low-loss play, but explain what its success removes and what remains. Authorize risk only on that named combat goal; retain ordinary limits elsewhere. Never call a predicted wipeout a winning option or claim final victory without confirmation.

# Town after capture

## When useful
Choose departure, return or renewed protection after capture or a completed hold.

## Facts and unknowns
Read actual income, stock/growth, buildings, funds, army pools, threats, delivery/return routes and remaining bases. Separate the garrison from the field army; purchases and conditional returns do not prove lasting safety.

## Alternatives to compare
Compare departure, funded garrison, interception and holding. Leave enough stationary force to deter observed weak raiders while preserving the strike army. For a base trade, compare income, recruitment, fortifications and development time. Unknown benefits of the new base do not offset confirmed losses of the main base. The trade should shorten the path to victory.

## Benefits, costs and risks
Before renewing a hold, compare its cumulative delay with the next offensive operation. Name the gain already obtained, why it did not unlock departure, and what specific improvement the next wait buys. A new deadline or another weekly refresh alone is insufficient. When preparation repeatedly fails, change the method or operation rather than renew the same promise.

Value the town's recurring income and affordable troop production against development, holding and delivery costs. Taking an enemy town may both strengthen own production and deny theirs; use observed facts, with future benefits conditional. Choose supported development, reinforcement or continued conquest that converts the capture into military advantage.

## When to reconsider
Recheck after stock, force, routes or ownership change. A completed hold needs a new operation. Use strategy_stalls to compare continued preparation with an executable route toward victory.

## Executor limits
Use supported prepare_garrison/intercept_hero participants and quotes. Retain needed garrison protection through the absence; confirm transfers and reprice the attack after force allocation. defense_exit states the observable exit condition and next operation.

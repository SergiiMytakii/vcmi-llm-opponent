# Town after capture

## When useful
Choose departure, return or renewed protection after capture or a completed hold.

## Facts and unknowns
Read actual stock, funds, army pools, threats, return routes and remaining bases. Separate the garrison from the field army; purchases and conditional returns do not prove lasting safety.

## Alternatives to compare
Compare departure, funded garrison, interception and holding. Leave enough stationary force to deter observed weak raiders while preserving the strike army. A useful base trade may justify exposure to the enemy main.

## Benefits, costs and risks
Before renewing a hold, compare its cumulative delay with the next offensive operation. Name the gain already obtained, why it did not unlock departure, and what specific improvement the next wait buys. A new deadline or another weekly refresh alone is insufficient. When preparation repeatedly fails, change the method or operation rather than renew the same promise.

## When to reconsider
Recheck after stock, force, routes or ownership change. A completed hold needs a new operation. Use strategy_stalls to compare continued preparation with an executable route toward victory.

## Executor limits
Use supported prepare_garrison/intercept_hero participants and quotes. Retain needed garrison protection through the absence; confirm transfers and reprice the attack after force allocation. defense_exit states the observable exit condition and next operation.

# Global strategy

## When useful
Choose the first saved course or reconsider it after a material native review signal.

## Facts and unknowns
Read victory conditions, map_overview coverage, own bases, forces, income preview and development options. Coordinates establish knowledge, not reachability. Omitted objects, hidden rewards, passage exits and enemy intentions remain unknown.

## Alternatives to compare
Compare 2-3 distinct admissible ways to win: production-led expansion, an achievable decisive offensive direction, or protection and recovery followed by renewed initiative. Name their benefit, cost and uncertainty; do not invent an extra option when fewer differ. A visible distant town, mine, ordinary artifact or passage can anchor a conditional future stage while the nearest operation uses current native quotes.

## Benefits, costs and risks
Keep one course with an objective, selection reason, evidence-backed uncertain assumptions and 3-6 measurable stages. Treat the first 2-3 weeks as an orientation; operations remain 3-7 days. Compare effective income with current building requirements and creature stock. Income previews assume current sources and exclude captures, finds and spending. Evaluate artifacts from supplied public effects and the native route cost.

## When to reconsider
Prefer keeping the course through a local delay, executor change, minor pickup or a single battle. Review confirmed target/actor loss, changed base exposure, blocked direction, absent progress or a completed stage. A revision explains the changed fact, transition cost in time, positions and prior purchases, and its contribution to victory. Ordinary alternatives compare the next operations.

## Executor limits
Native state and exact action receipts confirm stages. A historical capture is not current ownership; changing target, actor or value needs fresh proof. Supporting defense/logistics serves a stage without confirming it through an unrelated action. Use the reply schema and native instructions for the mandatory contract; this card supplies advice, not commands.

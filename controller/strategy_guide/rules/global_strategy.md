# Global strategy

## When useful
Choose or materially reconsider the saved victory course.

## Facts and unknowns
Read victory conditions, map size/coverage, bases, forces, income, production and native travel times. Known coordinates do not establish reachability; omitted objects and enemy intentions remain unknown.

## Alternatives to compare
Compare 2-3 supported victory courses by gain, time and loss risk. When victory is not immediately feasible, compare early offense with economic expansion: secure useful mines/towns, fund production and deliver a stronger army. Large maps and long travel times prompt this comparison; a nearby vulnerable enemy base may still favor immediate attack. Prefer the shortest credible winning course, not merely the earliest fight. Exact victory dates remain unknown.

## Benefits, costs and risks
Keep 3-6 measurable stages using supported predicates: useful mine/town ownership, a building present, army strength, then conquest. Economic preparation names the later offensive step it enables, the supported readiness condition and a review point. Compare recurring income and recruitable growth over several weeks where supported; mark longer projections conditional. Include holding costs, delivery and enemy gains while waiting. Wealth matters through usable military advantage, not accumulation alone.

## When to reconsider
Review threats, losses, blockage and stalled stages. Attach no_progress to unfinished conquest, not only army growth. Compare faster offense before renewing preparation; verified economic milestones can justify continued expansion when their military gain outweighs delay. Switch to offense when readiness is met or a decisive opening appears; reconsider expansion when its assets cannot be held or converted into useful force.

## Executor limits
Native state and receipts confirm progress. Historical capture is not current ownership. Quotes remain conditional; supporting defense does not complete conquest. Keep operative plans within 3-7 days and use the existing reply contract.
